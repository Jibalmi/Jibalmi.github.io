New and planned

New on 3 November 2026.

Changes to the harness that runs the agents, and to how it treats the disk. Each is new: written and tested on 3 Nov, not yet proven over a long run.

  1. newHarness · landing

    Reviewed work lands by itself

    When a task passes review, the lander finds the reviewed branch in whichever task checkout holds it and merges it. A stale, unmergeable edit in the way gives way to the landing, so the working copy matches the commit. Tasks that nothing alive is carrying any more are found and freed.

    3 Nov 2026·flow.ts

  2. newHarness · review fixes

    A fix loop stops at round 3, and steps up from round 2

    Before, 23 tasks on one project went past fix round 3 and one reached round 12. Now a task parked at the cap stays parked until its project manager decides, and from round 2 the fix worker runs one model class above the model that wrote the failing work, on the same subscription.

    3 Nov 2026·fix-cap-guard.ts · fix-round-floor.ts

  3. newAgents · method

    Every change carries its proof

    Workers prove each changed behaviour with a test that fails on the old code and passes on the new, and type-check only what they changed. Reviewers answer every acceptance line, and the title, MET or NOT MET with where it shows. The two largest review-failure causes over 14 days were weak tests (173 failures) and unmet acceptance lines (129).

    3 Nov 2026·worker-template.md

  4. newHarness · observer

    A standing observer, and a daily page reviewer

    Every 15 minutes an observer reads the task board and files what it finds, one task per problem: work no live agent carries, chains of blocked tasks, spikes of failures, agents idle beside a queue, finished work that never landed. Once a day, when it is switched on, a page reviewer screenshots the app’s pages at desktop and phone width in both themes and files each problem it sees.

    3 Nov 2026·harness-observer-arm.ts · page-reviewer-plan.ts

  5. newDisk safety

    Agents clean up after themselves

    On 3 Nov the drive filled because nothing owned the task checkouts: free space fell from 79 GB to 5 GB in seven hours, with 268 checkouts of about 0.44 GB each that had outlived their agents. Now the one who makes a checkout removes it. A task checkout is freed when its last session ends, and its branch keeps the work. Checkouts share a 25 GB budget, and over it no new one is made. A checkout leaves out the documentation’s images, videos and PDFs: about 280 MB instead of about 420 MB. A Claude Code session records the checkouts it makes and removes them when it ends; one that holds changes is kept, with the reason written down.

    3 Nov 2026·plan.ts · session-checkouts-clean.mjs

Planned

J-PEX: stop the loop at stages 4 and 5.

The hardware pipeline’s machine projects keep re-opening stages 4 (placement) and 5 (wiring), and stages 6 and 7 have never completed. The research reads why from the project records and orders the work by the waste it removes. From jpex-multimodal/roadmap.md.

The changeWhy, with its evidenceThe gate that proves it
J0Count a refiled stage as a revision, not a re-entry.52 of the 98 recorded re-entries were refiles, so half the churn number is bookkeeping and the rest is real rework.The 98 recorded rows replay as 52 refiles and 46 re-entries.
J1Solve placement and wiring as one installation problem with the proposers that already exist, for a bounded number of rounds.27 of the 34 stage-4 re-entries fell within an hour of a stage-5 re-entry. Stewards solved that loop by hand: 39 configuration and 35 arrangement calls, and no proposer call.On the next machine project, at most 2 re-entries of stages 4 and 5 per frozen installation, against 34 and 52 on Drone v2.
J2A measurement channel in the CAD jail: an authored program returns a small JSON dict of measurements and builds nothing.135 of 212 failed live CAD runs were agents raising an error only to read a measurement out, each costing a jail slot and a false failure row.A source that only measures returns its numbers and records no failure.
J3Optimise what binds: a declared stiffness ceiling, and only fine-solved candidates nominated.On the arm the strength margin sat far above its floor while stiffness binds; on the drone the same design solved fine dropped its safety factor from 3.281 to 2.522.An arm sweep with a deflection ceiling nominates a fine-solved design.
J4Let the generative loop spend its budget, then compare it with a sampler on real FEA under a gate it can meet.Given more time the in-house loop stopped after 17.5 fine solves on average, so its old gate could not be reached.Given more time, it spends that time and its result improves.
J5Close the physical loop on time and mass: the first print through the print door, weighed and timed, against the slicer’s prediction.No print has been measured yet: the records hold 202 print-ready rows and no physical reading.The first predicted-against-measured row for time and mass on one revision.

Planned

The twin, in its real environment.

Today the simulator has a ground plane and gravity, and no obstacle model. The plan places the whole machine in a reconstruction of its real site. The research ran the reconstruction, the collision split and the Monte Carlo in miniature on a laptop’s CPU, at no cost; a phone camera, a printed marker and calipers are the capture kit. From digital-twin/roadmap.md.

The changeWhy, with its evidenceThe gate that proves it
T1Scene input and arm-against-scene contact in the dynamics sidecar, with clearance recorded per pair.Turns “no obstacle model” into a measured clearance; the bench run repeats with an identical trajectory hash.A known-colliding run names the pair and the time.
T2Split every mesh that collides into convex parts (CoACD).Modelled as one hull, a pipe ring held a 40 mm ball on top at 0.34 m; split into 15 parts, the ball fell through to 0.04 m.That ring test as a sidecar test.
T3Monte Carlo over a register of unknowns, each with where its range came from (MuJoCo, on the CPU).300 runs without a failure bound the failure rate below one in a hundred; a nominal pass alone hides the thinnest margin.The same seeds give the same summary.
T4A verdict card for any requirement: pass, fail, marginal or unknown, with the margin, the failure bound and what is not modelled.Today twin readiness is three fixed cards and one point comparison.A fixture yields exactly pass, fail, marginal and unknown.
T5A site sidecar: photos, structure from motion (COLMAP on the CPU), a printed marker for scale, an independent check distance, a collision scene.30 synthetic phone views registered with no camera data, and one 150 mm marker set the scale. The run peaked at 11.5 GB of memory, so it needs a declared budget.On a real capture of a measured calibration block, the check distance and the block agree within 3 mm.
T6Reach in the scene, and cable slack over the joint sweep.Reach today ignores obstacles, and nothing checks a harness over motion.A target behind an obstacle reads unreachable, naming the obstacle.
T7Calibration from the first physical readings: damping, friction and motor constants fitted to a servo step response and friction coupons.No physical reading of any kind is on record yet; it needs the operator, an arm and an emergency stop.The calibrated twin predicts a held-out physical run within its own interval.
T8Write the uncertainty and calibration step into the design method.The method has no such step today.The operator’s ruling on the decision record.

Planned

A lab desk that guides the build.

A projector and a camera above the bench draw each step onto the desk itself: the parts to have in hand, where each one goes, and each connection one by one, paged by voice. It reuses the assembly manual the Build Lab already derives from a project’s parts. Nothing is built yet, and the desk itself can be filed as a J-PEX project. From lab-desk/roadmap.md.

The changeWhy, with its evidenceThe gate that proves it
Phase 0Measure the real desk, ceiling and light, and probe the printer’s port read-only.Every requirement row scales from these numbers.The numbers written into the project’s rows.
Phase 1One projector and one camera: calibration, height correction, a breadboard layout from the netlist, a projector page, hand tracking, a component detector trained on our own data, a resistor-band reader, desk commands with no model call, and privacy (a field-of-view mask, an indicator, nothing kept by default, nothing leaves the computer).In simulation, undistortion took the projected error from 5.5 mm to 0.03 mm; a stock detector found none of the desk’s components; bands read 300 of 300 synthetic resistors at 6 pixels per mm.The operator builds a small Arduino circuit from projected guidance alone; 35 projected crosses within 1.0 mm on a measured target.
Phase 2Scanning and measuring: a turntable, scans compared with the CAD, dimensions drawn around things on the desk.Wires the one core event that has no emitter today (scan against CAD).Scans within 0.5 mm on a reference bar.
Phase 3The arm and the printer: hand-aware arm speed, a printer interlock, and later a plate swap by arm.Needs hardware the operator has not bought; every write stays behind the existing arming rule.Force and stop timing measured before any unattended run.

The changeWhy, with its evidenceThe gate that proves it
R1Make the model lab admissible: clear the two marks that refuse every receipt, fill the speed fields, and run three seeds with an interval.About 3,000 receipts exist and none is admissible yet, and the speed fields are empty.Two models with admissible receipts on every suite, reproduced on another day.

R3Keep Ollama for the models it serves; add llama-server only where Ollama cannot do the job on this machine (speculative decoding, large mixture-of-experts models); put the stable part of every long prompt first.On the same weights Ollama decoded 127.0 tokens a second and llama.cpp 121.6. Planned

Models we build ourselves.

When the only good model or tool for a job carries a licence we cannot use, the plan is to build our own from the published paper, never from its code. The procedure keeps the spec writer, the implementer and the verifier in separate sessions; the implementer never opens upstream code, never loads restricted weights and never trains on restricted data; a patent search comes before the design. It is an engineering procedure, not legal advice, and a lawyer confirms it before anything ships. From own-build/roadmap.md.

The changeWhy, with its evidenceThe gate that proves it
O0Finish the patent searches and write a one-page brief for counsel.Some searches did not complete, and a live patent family can turn a planned build into a blocked one.Every row carries a completed search or a counsel answer.
O1Adopt the clean-room procedure as a decision record, with our own similarity checker.The rule against copying code needs a checkable procedure for models derived from papers.The checker flags a planted copied block and passes a rewrite.
O2Scan to CAD, stage A: a deterministic fitter from a point cloud to build123d.No permissively licensed point-to-CAD tool exists.High agreement with our own sampled parts, with every region it cannot explain reported.
O3An evaluation set built from the project’s own 639 authored CAD sources.A model is judged on our parts, not on a public set it may have seen.It reproduces the known ordering on a calibration subset.
O4A desk component detector trained on our own photos and renders (RF-DETR Nano, Apache-2.0).The available detectors know no electronics parts, and the common one carries the AGPL.The lab desk’s detection gate.
LaterA learned scan-to-CAD model of our own, on a 24 to 32 GB GPU node.Waits for that node, which the operator has not bought; rented GPUs are refused.It must beat stage A on held-out real scans.
ReconstructionFeed-forward scene reconstruction of the VGGT class.VGGT-1B’s weights are non-commercial, so the research recommends an Apache-licensed alternative (MapAnything) rather than a build; a marker-scaled COLMAP capture still sets every verdict.Adopted only after it beats COLMAP with a marker on the same capture.
